# Intent elicitation — teach the landscape before the user chooses

> reviewed: 2026-07-24 · tiers: the closed vocabulary in README rule 2 (the audit prints it on error)

Users with domain-knowledge gaps cannot articulate their real goal: they ask for an attempted solution, omit constraints they don't know exist (licensing, hardware, ops burden), and can't evaluate options framed as implementation attributes. This note grounds #290 — the `understand_intent` skill and its AskUserQuestion JIT reminder — and the candidate experiments below: the procedure that turns "answer the ask" into "equip an informed decision".

Provenance: two web sweeps by session research agents (2026-07-13/16 window); the raw sweeps with all sources are preserved verbatim as comments on #290. Accessed dates below are as stamped by the fetching agent.

## State of the art — LLM/agent research

- LM-led elicitation (open-ended questions + synthesized edge cases) beats user-written prompts on informativeness, costs users less effort, and surfaces considerations users had not anticipated — the documented "informed pivot" (GATE) [published] (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11589, accessed 2026-07-16)
- Rewarding replies by simulated downstream conversation (CollabLLM, ICML 2025 Outstanding Paper) organically produces clarifying behavior: +18.5% task performance, and in a 201-participant study +17.6% satisfaction with 10.4% less user time — elicitation repays the user's time [published] (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00640, accessed 2026-07-16)
- Ration questions by intent entropy: clarifying only the ~10% highest-entropy cases doubles the gain vs randomly chosen clarifications — ask only where interpretations diverge in consequences [published] (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09469, accessed 2026-07-16)
- Information-gain-priced questions cost ~0.3 extra interaction steps for +3.7% success — well-targeted asking is nearly free [published] (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03135, accessed 2026-07-16; the abstract page lists ICML 2026 — confirm at camera-ready)
- The default tool-agent failure is silently inventing unstated arguments; ask-when-needed outperforms on Noisy ToolBench [published] (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.00557, accessed 2026-07-16)
- Underspecification detection decoupled from execution resolves 69.4% of underspecified SWE-bench Verified, approaching fully-specified performance — detecting intent gaps and executing are different jobs [published] (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26233, accessed 2026-07-16)
- Ask-vs-answer improves when replies are judged by simulating future turns across plausible intents (+5% F1) — the single-interpretation answer is a single-turn-training artifact, not correct behavior [published] (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13788, accessed 2026-07-16)
- Answer-first interaction causes premature convergence on a narrow idea set; generating the design-space *dimensions* first (Luminate) is the closest academic form of teach-the-landscape [published] (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12953, accessed 2026-07-16)
- Two-bucket triage: resolve researchable gaps yourself; ask only the preference gaps the user alone holds, batched at plan level; Claude's self-initiated check-in rate roughly doubles on complex tasks [production-proven] (source: https://www.anthropic.com/research/trustworthy-agents, accessed 2026-07-16)
- Proactivity has a civility dimension — unchecked initiative-taking reads as intrusive; adapt teaching/questioning depth to the user's signals [published] (source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.12670, accessed 2026-07-16)
- Non-experts systematically fail to articulate intent as instructions (ad-hoc, over-generalized from single examples) — treat the first message as evidence about intent, not as the spec [published] (source: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3544548.3581388, accessed 2026-07-16)

## State of the art — practitioner disciplines

- XY problem: users ask about their attempted solution, not their goal; the remedy is probing "what does this get you?" before executing [production-proven] (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XY_problem, accessed 2026-07-13)
- "What do you want?" collects feature fragments; "what do you need to *do* with it?" — task walkthroughs including exception paths — yields the real requirements (Wiegers) [production-proven] (source: https://www.informit.com/articles/article.aspx?p=3172445, accessed 2026-07-13)
- Jobs-to-be-Done: restate the ask as a job-in-a-circumstance ("when X, I want Y, so that Z") and ask what the user hires for it today; the milkshake case's winning changes had nothing to do with the requested improvements [production-proven] (source: https://hbr.org/2016/09/know-your-customers-jobs-to-be-done, accessed 2026-07-13)
- Kano: must-be needs are structurally unstated — users assume the baseline and only its absence registers, as anger; enumerate the taken-for-granted baseline yourself [published] (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kano_model, accessed 2026-07-13)
- Shared decision-making (three-talk model, BMJ): announce that a choice exists *before* presenting options; elicit "what matters most to you?" as an explicit step between options and recommendation [published] (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5683042/, accessed 2026-07-13)
- Option Grids: one-page comparison tables whose rows are the lived-consequence questions patients actually ask (recovery time, what daily life looks like), plain language — the closest existing artifact to end-state trade-off framing [published] (source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_grid, accessed 2026-07-13)
- SHARE adds Evaluate — revisit the decision after it ships — and Purposeful SDM adds: classify the problem *type* first; not every problem is an options-comparison [published] (source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8142549/, accessed 2026-07-13)
- Commander's intent (purpose · key tasks · end state) lets execution drop the plan without dropping the goal — carry the intent, not the ask [published] (source: https://fieldgradeleader.themilitaryleader.com/cdr-intent/, accessed 2026-07-13)
- MDMP fixes **evaluation criteria before the options are compared** — the March 2010 FM 5-0 revision moved criteria development into mission analysis, ahead of COA development — and the staff *recommends* while the commander *decides* [experimental] (source: https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0effe0a4-a327-4773-aac4-234ece006e34/content, accessed 2026-07-24). Tier rationale: the fetched source is Nunn's 2010 UT Austin master's report, which quotes FM 5-0 (Department of the Army, March 2010) directly — secondary, not doctrine itself, which still returned 403/404 across armypubs, globalsecurity, and FAS mirrors on 2026-07-13, 2026-07-16, and again 2026-07-24. **Deliberately not claimed, because no fetched source supports either:** that the ordering exists to *avoid bias*, and that the weighted matrix is *explicitly advisory* — both were search-summary-only, and they are now Watch re-check questions (#564).
- Mom Test: ban hypothetical validation — ask for the last concrete instance of the problem and what it cost, never "would you use X?" [production-proven] (source: https://www.unusual.vc/rob-fitzpatricks-mom-test/, accessed 2026-07-13)
- SPIN implication questions: chain "and what does that lead to?" until the user states the stakes in their own words — a solution offered against an implied need gets politely ignored [production-proven] (source: https://www.highspot.com/blog/spin-selling/, accessed 2026-07-13)
- The Ford "faster horse" quote is apocryphal — never cite it as real (source: https://quoteinvestigator.com/2011/07/28/ford-faster-horse/, accessed 2026-07-13)

## Gaps the literature does not cover

- Teach-before-ask ordering ("users decide better when briefed on the landscape before choosing") — no direct test found in the literature; **tested here as EXP-06 leg 2 (2026-07-18): REFUTED, inverted** — ask-first won 6/8 blind passes on the identical artifact set (pKQ 0.25); judges read the pre-elicitation recommended default as anchoring. The #414 reorder shipped ask-first and **EXP-07 validated it as the shipped file (pRT 0.75, all duels unanimous)**. (Full write-up lives in the template's RR-01 report, not carried here — #627 port rule.) **Boundary — `EXP-11` (2026-07-23, #444): the ask-first advantage INVERTS under non-compliant owners** — re-running EXP-07's protocol with owners who deflect/mis-answer/resent questions (hidden facts unlocking only on teaching a dimension or a specific well-targeted ask), teach-first won all 8 blind order-swapped passes (pRT 0.00 vs 0.75 on compliant owners), zero flips; firing questions first at a resistant owner under-surfaces owner-held facts and reads as interrogation, while teaching first still unlocks a reluctant owner. The #414 ordering's benefit is **contingent on owner compliance** — validated for the common compliant case, bounded here for resistant owners (*ask-first wins when the owner will answer; when the owner resists, teach first*). **The boundary is robust to turn budget — `EXP-12` (2026-07-24, #542): the shipped ask-first skill does NOT recover given a third turn** — re-running EXP-11's non-compliant protocol at a 3-turn shape (A1 ask → O1 deflect → A2 re-ask/pivot → O2 → A3 final), teach-first again won all 8 blind order-swapped passes (pR3 0.00, unchanged from the two-turn 0.00), zero flips; the transcripts show the shipped skill front-loads its question batch, draws the volume-deflection, then does not use the recovery turn to re-probe (A2/A3 question counts collapse to ≤2) — it proceeds to solutioning, so the load-bearing owner-held fact stays hidden in every scenario (and on the pivot sub-score the extra turn produced a *more* confidently under-informed plan, 0.875 vs EXP-11's 1.50). The "just give it more turns" free lunch is ruled out. This *motivates* candidate 2 (a resistance-detection → lead-with-teaching step-5 clause), filed as its own EXP `#545` — a new treatment validated **before** any skill edit. **That validation ran and failed — `EXP-13` (2026-07-24, #545): the clause does NOT restore parity (pR13 0.25, bar ≥0.5; zero flips), and the pre-committed discriminator makes the refutation sharper than the pooled number** — the clause *fired* in 3 of 4 scenarios (literal `resistance:` line) and scored **0.0000 across all 6 of those passes**, EXP-12's exact floor, while the entire +0.25 came from the one scenario where it correctly stayed silent. It was not inert: it shrank the opening batch by roughly a third (A1 question-count mean 10.5 → 7.25) and restored the A2 re-probe (`[0,0,1,0]` → `[1,0,3,1]`), i.e. it corrected EXP-12's named mechanism precisely and owner outcomes did not follow. **The deficit is the ordering, not the missing re-probe** — a dimension taught *after* a failed interrogation does not buy what the same dimension taught *first* buys, because the owner has already been given a reason to disengage. The shipped skill is therefore **unchanged**; candidate 2 is foreclosed as this clause. Across EXP-11/12/13 the whole "let ask-first cope with resistance" family is ruled out by turn budget and by post-hoc repair; the remaining live hypothesis is **routing** — detect the resistant owner *before* the first ask — which none of the three tested, since all three fix the ordering at turn 1.
- Recommended-default-first option presentation (our decision-template posture) — no direct study found. Nearest support: mixed-initiative expected-utility act-vs-ask (source: http://erichorvitz.com/chi99horvitz.pdf, accessed 2026-07-16 — existence search-confirmed, content lightly verified) and the MDMP staff-recommends posture above. EXP-06 leg 2 added adjacent evidence — a recommended default delivered *before* elicitation was consistently docked as anchoring — which cautioned on timing, not the posture. **Tested directly as `EXP-10` (2026-07-23, #445): SUPPORTED** — with elicitation done first, leading the brief with a recommended default did not degrade the blind simulated-owner best-fit rate (1.000 vs 0.958 neutral, delta +0.042 = a tie at ceiling) and blind judges preferred it in all 8 duel passes with zero flips; the one owner who chose a poor-fit option did so in the *neutral* arm. The anchoring caution does not extend to a default delivered *after* elicitation when the recommendation is reliable (mechanism: recommendation accuracy 4 of 4). The rule stands, no decision change. Residual — default-first when the recommendation is *wrong* — `#539`.
- End-state-consequence framing of trade-offs (vs attribute framing) — untested in LLM-agent settings; decision-science attribute-framing literature not swept.

## Template feasibility path

The D-137 pattern applies — canon + skill + validating EXP — with the amendment this repo's own experiments earned: EXP-03/04 showed canon alone transfers taste but not behavior (the enforced gate loop closed the gap), and the forced-artifact authoring rule (a rule held 0/4 runs as prose, 4/4 as a required report line — `.claude/skills/README.md` › authoring) says the same about weak executors. So the capture is a procedure with required artifacts (`understand_intent`): job restatement · dimensions map (incl. the self-enumerated must-be baseline) · criteria-before-options · consequence grid (lived-consequence rows) · what-matters-most elicitation · dissolve check — gated by intent entropy so low-stakes converging asks skip the ceremony, with the AskUserQuestion JIT reminder as the recency-riding pointer (#252 pattern; timing honesty in the hook header). The decision template's Options section carries the consequence framing so the grid pastes straight in.

## Candidate experiments

- Teach-before-ask ablation: same knowledge-gap scenario (the artist/asset-pipeline golden), briefing-then-question vs question-first; blind judge scores "could the user have made an informed decision from this transcript?" — **run as EXP-06 leg 2: refuted, inverted (pKQ 0.25); fix validated as shipped by EXP-07 (pRT 0.75)**. **EXP-11 extended this to non-compliant owners: INVERTED again (pRT 0.00) — ask-first's advantage does not survive owners who resist elicitation; teach-first wins 8/8. EXP-12 (#542) then answered the recovery question: NO — a third turn does not rescue the shipped ask-first skill (pR3 0.00, teach-first 8/8 again); it front-loads its batch, draws deflection, and proceeds without re-probing. Candidate 2 (a resistance-detection → lead-with-teaching clause) is thereby motivated and filed as EXP `#545`, validated before any edit — and **EXP-13 (#545) refuted it: NO EFFECT (pR13 0.25), scoring 0.0000 on the six passes where the clause actually fired. Shipped skill unchanged; the repair-after-resistance family is closed, and only pre-ask routing remains untested.**
- ±skill transfer: weaker-model sessions with and without `understand_intent` on the same scenarios, same blind judge — the EXP-03-shaped canon-transfer question, for elicitation. **Run as EXP-06 leg 1: supported at the maximum (pKB 1.00 on Sonnet executors, four scenarios unanimous)**.
- Recommended-default-first vs neutral presentation: informed-decision score + owner turn count. **Run as `EXP-10` (2026-07-23): SUPPORTED** — default-first did not degrade best-fit choice and judges preferred it in all 8 passes; owner confidence up (4.71 vs 4.25) and follow-ups down (0.125 vs 0.417) at no accuracy cost. Bounded to the reliable-recommendation regime (accuracy 4 of 4); residual `#539`.
